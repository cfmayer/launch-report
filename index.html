The Week in Launch

One launch since Monday; Vulcan returns November 13

The only orbital launch worldwide since Monday's issue was SpaceX's Starlink 15-16, which left Vandenberg at 3:25 a.m. EDT Tuesday. That brings 2026 to 251 orbital launch attempts, 244 of them successful, according to GCAT. ISRO's GSLV with the NVS-03 navigation satellite is set for 2:21 p.m. Friday, and Starlink 15-18 follows from Vandenberg late Friday night.

United Launch Alliance says Vulcan will return to flight on Friday, November 13, carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites, after a nine-month grounding over solid rocket booster nozzle problems. On the International Space Station, NASA's Anil Menon and the Canadian Space Agency's Joshua Kutryk began a spacewalk at 8:13 a.m. Thursday to install a new solar array.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

Starlink 15-16 flies on a 26th-flight booster

Falcon 9 lifted off 2 hours 40 minutes into its window and landed its booster in the Pacific.

Falcon 9: Starlink 15-16

Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base at 3:25 a.m. EDT Tuesday (07:25 UTC; GCAT), 12:25 a.m. local time, Spaceflight Now posted. Monday's issue listed the start of the FAA airspace window, 12:45 a.m. EDT (FAA). Booster B1082 was on its 26th flight (NASASpaceflight). The booster entered service in January 2024 and has also flown the USSF-62, OneWeb Launch 20, NROL-145 and Transporter-18 missions plus 21 Starlink batches; it landed on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You off Baja California, Mexico, and the rocket headed southeast toward an orbit inclined 70 degrees (Spaceflight Now).

"Deployment of 27 @Starlink satellites confirmed," SpaceX posted at 4:29 a.m. It was SpaceX's 122nd launch of the year (GCAT).

Elsewhere

No other orbital launches have taken place worldwide since Saturday night's Long March 8A from Wenchang, which Monday's issue covered (Launch Library 2; GCAT). Rocket Lab said its 98th Electron, for HawkEye 360 last Friday, will be followed by its 99th launch next week (Rocket Lab).

IV · Reentries & ISS Traffic

A 1973 French satellite comes down after 52 years

CelesTrak lists Aureole 2, five Starlinks and four other objects as decayed since Monday's issue.

Station traffic: a seventh roll-out array

"The spacewalk is now underway!" NASA posted at 8:13 a.m. Thursday. NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk are to install and unfurl the seventh of eight International Space Station Roll-Out Solar Arrays, or IROSAs (Spaceflight Now), in a spacewalk planned to last about six and a half hours (NASA). Spacewalk preparations and robotics work filled Tuesday for the Expedition 75 crew (NASA).

The next visitors are unchanged: Progress MS-36 at 12:03 a.m. EST November 24 from Baikonur, Cygnus NG-25 in November and China's Shenzhou 24 to Tiangong NET November 25 (Launch Library 2).

V · Industry & Regulation

Vulcan returns to flight with six boosters and fixed nozzles

ULA's November 13 flight carries Amazon's largest Leo payload yet, and Rocket Lab's Neutron hardware reached its Virginia pad.

United Launch Alliance

The launch of a Vulcan VC6L "carrying the next batch of production satellites for @AmazonLeo, Vulcan Leo 1, is planned for Friday, Nov. 13, pending range approval," ULA posted Wednesday. "All 40 Leo satellites—our largest payload to date—have been integrated with Vulcan inside Amazon's vertical integration facility (VIF-A) at the Cape," Amazon Leo posted.

It will be Vulcan's fifth flight and its first with six strap-on solid rocket boosters, after a nine-month grounding. Nozzle failures on the Northrop Grumman-built boosters marred its second flight and, in February, its fourth, though both reached their intended orbits, Spaceflight Now reported. "This upcoming launch will utilize the improved nozzle design, and additional testing and analysis will continue in parallel with the mission," ULA said in a statement quoted by Spaceflight Now. Amazon has booked 38 Vulcan launches, nearly 400 Leo satellites are in orbit, and a second vehicle is already assembled for Vulcan Leo 2 (Spaceflight Now).

Rocket Lab

"Neutron's Stage 2 and Thrust Module have arrived to Virginia," Rocket Lab posted Wednesday; after a barge ride from its Neutron factory they were offloaded on Wallops Island, with integrated fluids testing and launch-mount checkouts next. The company said its 100th Electron is "scheduled for delivery to Launch Complex 1 soon" (post), that eight of 18 Tranche 2 spacecraft for the Space Development Agency are in build in California (post), and that its solar panels on NASA's NEO Surveyor sunshade deliver up to 2 kilowatts, the spacecraft's only power source (post).

Starship

Ship 43 has returned to the production site from chopstick tests at Pad 2, and Booster 23 was set to head to Massey's for cryogenic testing; the pair is expected to sail to Kennedy Space Center for the first Starship flight from Pad 39A, Flight 16, NASASpaceflight reported.

Quick Hit

Artemis III. "The core stage of the Artemis III rocket is officially stacked," NASA's Artemis account posted Wednesday.

Europe and India

ESA's Hera "can now see Didymos and Dimorphos as two separate asteroids," the agency posted Tuesday; the first detailed images of Dimorphos are expected on November 25. ESA has ordered four more Galileo Second Generation satellites, bringing the total to 16 (ESA), and signed three contracts in Norway on GNSS interference testing, maritime navigation and drones (post). ISRO set GSLV-F18 for 23:51 India time Friday, 2:21 p.m. EDT (post).

Comment deadlines

New Glenn at SLC-36. Comments on raising New Glenn's cap from 12 to 50 launches a year close Friday, October 30 (notice, docket FAA-2026-9841).

Part 450. Comments on five proposed changes to the commercial launch and reentry licensing rule are due November 4 (docket FAA-2026-9935).

FCC space licensing. Comments on the Space Modernization for the 21st Century further notice (SB Docket No. 25-306) are due November 25 and replies December 28 (Federal Register).

Starbase. Scoping comments on up to 456 Starship launches a year at Starbase are due November 30, with meetings November 17 to 19 (Federal Register).

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 251 orbital launch attempts through Tuesday's Starlink 15-16, with 244 successes. SpaceX has flown 122, all successful, followed by China with 76, Rocket Lab with 17 and Russia with 13; Europe has flown seven, other U.S. providers nine, and India and Japan three each.

The failures

According to GCAT, seven launches have failed this year: India's PSLV on January 12; China's Long March 3B on January 16, Ceres-2 on January 17, Tianlong-3 on April 3 and Long March 7A on August 10; Japan's Kairos on March 5; and Blue Origin's New Glenn on April 19, recorded as a partial failure. Galactic Energy's Ceres-2 is listed to fly again on November 4 (Launch Library 2).

October so far

Ten of this month's 19 orbital launches were SpaceX Falcons (GCAT). Vandenberg has hosted six of them, more than any other site, and Cape Canaveral and Kennedy four between them. China has flown six, while South Korea's Nuri, Japan's H3 and Rocket Lab's Electron account for the other three.
