The Week in Launch

Electron's 99th and a Spacesail cluster open November

Two orbital launches flew worldwide since Monday's issue, both successful. Rocket Lab's Electron lofted a Japanese radar satellite on its 99th flight Monday morning from Mahia, and China's Kinetica 2 put 18 Spacesail communications satellites into polar orbit Monday evening from Jiuquan. That brings 2026 to 255 orbital launch attempts, 248 of them successful, according to GCAT plus Monday's flights from Launch Library 2.

USSF-432 has slipped again, to Friday night in the FAA plan, and Starlink 15-21 is off the near-term board. NASA's Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory reentered Wednesday over Indonesia after nearly 22 years, and Canadian and NASA astronauts were due outside the International Space Station this morning to install the final roll-out solar array.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

Electron hits 99; Kinetica 2 flies Spacesail's polar group

Rocket Lab's 18th orbital launch of the year and China's second Kinetica 2 both reached orbit.

Electron: "The Sea God Surveys"

"We've officially launched Electron 99 times," Rocket Lab posted after the flight. "It took us seven years to reach 50. Now, just two years later, we're closing in on 100." Liftoff was at 11:41 a.m. EST Monday (16:41 UTC) from Launch Complex 1A on New Zealand's Mahia Peninsula, the opening of a two-hour window (NASASpaceflight; Spaceflight Now). The rocket put QPS-SAR-20, also called WADATSUMI-II, into low Earth orbit for Japan's Institute for Q-shu Pioneers of Space on the company's 10th dedicated Electron mission and first attempt of the day (Rocket Lab).

"Mission success! Repeat customer @QPS_Inc has made contact with its latest QPS-SAR satellite following their tenth ride on Electron," the company confirmed later Monday. It was Electron's 17th flight of 2026 and Rocket Lab's 18th orbital launch of the year per GCAT.

Kinetica 2: SpaceSail Polar Group #17

CAS Space's Kinetica 2 lifted off at about 7:15 p.m. EST Monday (00:15 UTC Tuesday) from Launch Area 140 at Jiuquan and reached orbit with 18 low-Earth-orbit communications satellites for Shanghai Spacesail Technologies' G60 constellation, according to Launch Library 2. The satellites carry Ku-, Q- and V-band payloads; Spacesail plans 1,296 satellites by 2027. It was the second flight of Kinetica 2 after its March debut (NASASpaceflight). No other orbital launches took place between Monday's pair and Thursday morning (Launch Library 2).

IV · Reentries & ISS Traffic

Swift comes down over Indonesia; final iROSA spacewalk today

NASA's gamma-ray observatory ended nearly 22 years in orbit Wednesday, and two astronauts were due outside the station this morning.

Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory

Swift "plummeted back into Earth's atmosphere Wednesday over western Indonesia," Spaceflight Now reported, ending a mission that launched Nov. 20, 2004, on a Delta II. U.S. Space Force tracking, later confirmed by NASA, put reentry at 7:39 a.m. EST Wednesday (12:39 UTC) near 3 degrees north, 99 degrees east, over North Sumatra. NASA said most of the spacecraft was expected to burn up, with a roughly 1-in-2,100 chance that surviving fragments could harm anyone on the ground.

Designed for two years, Swift observed more than 2,000 gamma-ray bursts and more than 1,400 supernovae and contributed to about 7,000 scientific papers. A $30 million Katalyst Space robotic tug intended to raise its orbit lost control after a July 3 launch; NASA called off the capture Aug. 19. Swift made its final observation Oct. 20 (Spaceflight Now).

Station traffic: the last roll-out array

Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk and NASA astronaut Anil Menon "will begin a spacewalk expected to last about seven hours" at 8:30 a.m. EST Thursday to "install the final roll-out solar array," the space station account posted this morning. Menon called it "installment 7 of 8," noting last week's array produced a greater than 40 percent power increase on its channel (NASA). Wednesday's Expedition 75 work finished spacewalk preparations (NASA).

The next visitors remain Progress MS-36 at 12:03 a.m. November 24 from Baikonur, China's Shenzhou 24 to Tiangong NET late November, and Cygnus NG-25 at 10:49 a.m. November 29 (Launch Library 2).

V · Industry & Regulation

Vulcan Leo 1 holds Nov. 13; Hubble reboost RFI still open

Rocket Lab's ESCAPADE probes near a Mars burn, ULA's return to flight keeps its date, and Isaacman presses for a controlled Hubble end of life.

Rocket Lab and ESCAPADE

"Get ready: Trans-Mars Injection incoming," Rocket Lab posted Monday about Blue and Gold, the twin Explorer-class probes it built for NASA's ESCAPADE mission with the University of California, Berkeley. "Our two spacecraft … will slingshot Earth and shoot for Mars in four days." A follow-up Tuesday walked through the in-house solar panels, star trackers and reaction wheels as the probes neared the critical burn (Rocket Lab). Separately, Maxwell, a Rocket Lab-built spacecraft for Viasat under NASA's Communications Services Project, returned one of its first on-orbit images and is "healthy and moving through commissioning ahead of schedule" (Rocket Lab).

United Launch Alliance

Vulcan Leo 1, carrying 40 Amazon Leo satellites on a VC6L with six solid rocket boosters, remains planned for Friday, November 13, "pending range approval" (ULA). Launch Library 2 now carries a 12:54 p.m. EST T-0. The flight is Vulcan's return after nozzle failures on the Northrop Grumman 63XL motors grounded the rocket following the February USSF-87 mission; ULA says the upcoming launch uses an improved nozzle design (Spaceflight Now). A second Vulcan is already stacked for Vulcan Leo 2.

Hubble Request for Information

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman told NASASpaceflight that an uncontrolled Hubble reentry "would not be the right thing to do," and that any funded visit should reboost the telescope to extend its science return (NASASpaceflight). Hubble is operating on two of six gyroscopes in single-gyro mode, cutting available observation time by about 12 percent; without propulsion it is expected to reenter around 2033–2034. NASA's Request for Information on commercial options for a reboost, servicing or controlled deorbit remains open through the end of November. Isaacman cited the failed Swift rescue as useful experience for any future capture attempt.

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 253 orbital launch attempts through October 31. Adding Monday's Electron and Kinetica 2 from Launch Library 2 brings the running total to 255 attempts and 248 successes. SpaceX remains at 123, all successful, followed by China with 77, Rocket Lab with 18 and Russia with 13; other U.S. providers have flown nine, Europe seven, India four and Japan three. GCAT counts Japan's October 19 launch of the Martian Moons eXploration probe as a deep-space launch, outside these totals.

Galactic Energy's Ceres-2 is listed to fly again Friday (Launch Library 2).
