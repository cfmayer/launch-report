The Week in Launch

MMX is on its way to Mars; three launches, three successes since Monday

Japan's H3 sent the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe toward Mars at 3:41 p.m. EDT Monday, a Falcon 9 flew the Bandwagon-5 rideshare from Florida on Tuesday morning, and Galactic Energy launched a Ceres-1S from the sea off eastern China that night. That brings 2026 to 246 orbital launch attempts, 239 of them successful, counting GCAT through October 15 and Launch Library 2 since.

Two launches stood down. Thunderstorms scrubbed Starlink 15-15 at Vandenberg on Wednesday, a day after a technical issue stopped it, and Rocket Lab postponed an Electron for HawkEye 360 to review "out-of-family telemetry data." Both have new dates this week, as do three Long March rockets.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

H3 flies MMX on time, to plan

The Phobos sample-return probe separated 71 minutes after liftoff and is due at Mars in August 2027.

H3-24: MMX

The H3 lifted off from Tanegashima Space Center at 3:41 p.m. EDT Monday (19:41 UTC). The second stage and MMX reached a parking orbit 10 minutes 40 seconds after liftoff, and the 4,480-kilogram spacecraft separated at 1 hour 11 minutes 12 seconds, on a 10-month trajectory that reaches Mars in August 2027, NASASpaceflight reported. The separation time matches the one in the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency's (JAXA) launch plan. JAXA announced at 1:10 a.m. EDT Wednesday that MMX had completed its initial critical operations phase.

MMX carries 13 science instruments, among them NASA's MEGANE gamma-ray and neutron spectrometer and France's MIRS infrared spectrometer, and will release IDEFIX, a French-German rover, onto Phobos (NASASpaceflight). The P-Sampler mounted on one leg of the MMX lander will collect surface material with high-pressure nitrogen gas, Blue Origin, which supplies it, posted.

Falcon 9: Bandwagon-5

Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral at 7:04 a.m. EDT Tuesday (Launch Library 2) with 32 rideshare payloads, a day late because of recovery-zone weather. Booster B1085 landed on A Shortfall of Gravitas on its 19th flight. The stack carried SpaceX's second Starfall reentry capsule, 3.1 meters wide and able to carry 1,000 kilograms, plus four Xona Space Systems navigation satellites, Impulse Space's LEO Express 4 space tug, Planet's Pelican-12 imager and radar satellites from ICEYE, Capella Space and Umbra, NASASpaceflight reported. Rocket Lab said Wednesday that the Maxwell spacecraft it built for Viasat, which launched from Cape Canaveral on Tuesday, is in orbit (post).

Ceres-1S: Xiguang-4 01

Galactic Energy's Ceres-1S launched at 11:39 p.m. EDT Tuesday from the sea off Rizhao, Shandong, carrying Xiguang-4 01, a 0.5-meter-resolution Earth observation satellite for the Guangzhou government and Guangzhou University (Launch Library 2). It was the first mission run from a new local command center rather than the Taiyuan launch center, and Galactic Energy has now had 23 successes in 26 launches, NASASpaceflight reported.

III · Trajectories & Hazard Areas

Airspace windows for two Starlinks and a Mojave flight

The FAA's operations plan lists the times U.S. airspace is set aside; no hazard-area coordinates have been published for these flights.

How MMX flew

H3 Flight 10, the H3-24L version, followed the eastward ascent in JAXA's plan, using two LE-9 hydrogen engines, four solid boosters and the long fairing. MMX separated at 1 hour 11 minutes 12 seconds, at 1:11:12 in the plan's timeline, after a parking-orbit insertion at 10 minutes 40 seconds, NASASpaceflight reported. The plan's drop zones for the boosters, fairing and first stage were mapped in Monday's issue.

Bandwagon-5 followed a southeasterly track from Cape Canaveral, with payload deployment beginning 57 minutes 18 seconds after liftoff and the Starfall capsule scheduled to separate last, at 2 hours 48 minutes 28 seconds (NASASpaceflight). Initial Starfall tests are planned to splash down in the Pacific about 1,300 kilometers off the United States or Mexico.

V · Industry & Regulation

China prepares its first booster reflight

A recovered Long March 10B stage is being readied to fly again, and Long March 12A will try another landing.

China

The Long March 10B first stage that was caught at sea by a net aboard the recovery ship Linghangzhe on July 10 is being refurbished in Tianjin and is expected to become China's first orbital-class booster to be reused. One of two new commercial pads in Hainan is to make its first launch, a Long March 10B, before the end of the year, and that flight is expected, though not officially confirmed, to use the recovered stage, NASASpaceflight reported. The second Long March 12A, NET November 3, will try to land its first stage after the first attempt crashed about five kilometers from its target when one of three engines did not restart.

CAS Space may fly its second three-core Lijian-2 before the end of October with 18 Qianfan broadband satellites, and a second Qingzhou cargo craft is to launch on Lijian-2 to the Tiangong station no earlier than January 2027 with up to 1,800 kilograms of supplies. Chinese orbital launches are up nearly 14% from this point last year (NASASpaceflight).

More Chinese launches are moving to sea. CAS Space is preparing the first seaborne Lijian-1, from the ship Dongfang Hangtiangang, which is to sail south for a launch off Guangdong in the South China Sea. Beijing-based i-Space expects to static-fire the first stage of its Shuang Quxian-3 before the end of the year and plans a droneship landing attempt on the rocket's first flight in early 2027, while Cosmoleap has been testing a Starship-style catch tower for its Yueqian-1 (NASASpaceflight).

Both Chinese broadband constellations are nearing service. The China Satellite Network Group's Guowang network reached 222 satellites with an October 9 launch and is trialling service, and Shanghai Spacesail plans to start Qianfan service in China by the end of the year (NASASpaceflight).

Rocket Lab and Firefly

Rocket Lab has rotated the Neutron second-stage tank assembly horizontal, "a critical step in the handling of large rocket sections for integration and assembly," and is building a new carbon-composite forward dome for the first-stage tank (post). Firefly Aerospace has completed the preliminary design review for its part in NASA's MoonFall mission, in which its Elytra spacecraft will deploy drones built by NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory above the Moon's south pole (post).

Quick Hit

Blue and Gold. Next month the twin Mars spacecraft Rocket Lab built for NASA and the University of California, Berkeley, will swing past Earth and each fire its main engine in a single, continuous burn, using Earth's gravity, the company posted.

Europe

The 350th ESA Council appointed Andrzej Banasiak as Director of Commercialisation and Industry Partnership and Nicolaus Hanowski as Director of Space Operations, both from January 1, 2027, the European Space Agency posted Thursday. ESA is also developing OASIS, a lunar "service station" to provide power, communications and data to experiments through the two-week lunar night (post).

Comment deadlines

New Glenn at SLC-36. The FAA's virtual public meeting on raising New Glenn's cap from 12 to 50 launches a year is tonight from 6 to 8 p.m. EDT; comments close October 30 (notice, docket FAA-2026-9841).

Part 450. Comments on five proposed changes to the commercial launch and reentry licensing rule are due November 4 (docket FAA-2026-9935).

Starbase. Scoping comments on up to 456 Starship launches a year at Starbase are due November 30, with meetings November 17 to 19 (Federal Register). No new FAA launch notices have appeared since Monday (Federal Register).

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 243 orbital launch attempts through USSF-481 on October 15; the three launches since, logged by Launch Library 2, bring the year to 246, with 239 successes. SpaceX has flown 120, all successful, followed by China with 73, Rocket Lab with 16 and Russia with 13. Japan's MMX launch was its fourth of the year.

The failures

According to GCAT, seven launches have failed this year: India's PSLV on January 12; China's Long March 3B on January 16, Ceres-2 on January 17, Tianlong-3 on April 3 and Long March 7A on August 10; Japan's Kairos on March 5; and Blue Origin's New Glenn on April 19, recorded as a partial failure.

From the sea

Tuesday's Ceres-1S flight was among eight successful Chinese orbital launches from sea platforms counted so far this year, using Jielong-3, Yinli-1 and Ceres-1S rockets. China's launch total is up 48% from the same point in 2024 (NASASpaceflight).
