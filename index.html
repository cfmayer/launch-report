The Week in Launch

Five launches, five successes since Thursday

Rocket Lab's Electron put three HawkEye 360 satellites in orbit early Friday, China flew three Long March rockets from Taiyuan, Jiuquan and Wenchang between Friday morning and Saturday night, and Starlink 15-15 finally left Vandenberg on Friday evening after three earlier scrubs. That brings 2026 to 250 orbital launch attempts, 243 of them successful, according to GCAT.

Two Vandenberg Starlinks have moved a day later, India's GSLV now has a firm date of Friday, and United Launch Alliance's Vulcan with Amazon Leo LV-01 has slipped from late October into November. In Texas, Starship's Booster 22 completed a full-duration static fire on Sunday ahead of Flight 15.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

Starlink 15-15 flies on the fourth try

Electron and Falcon 9 each delivered on Friday, and China launched 16 satellites on three Long Marches.

Electron: "We're Not Hawking Around"

The 98th Electron lifted off from Launch Complex 1 on New Zealand's Mahia Peninsula at 3:11 a.m. EDT Friday (07:11 UTC; Launch Library 2) with three HAWK radio-frequency-sensing satellites for HawkEye 360, bound for a 520-kilometer orbit inclined 97 degrees (Rocket Lab). At 4:08 a.m., Rocket Lab posted: "Payload deployment confirmed from Electron - all three HAWK satellites for @hawkeye360 are now on orbit" (post). The flight, delayed from October 21 for a telemetry review, launched on its first attempt and was Rocket Lab's 19th mission of the year and its third for HawkEye 360, the company said.

Long March 6A: Yaogan-50 03

A Long March 6A, which Xinhua called a modified Long March-6, launched from Taiyuan at 6:15 a.m. EDT Friday (10:15 UTC) and placed Yaogan-50 03 in its planned orbit. Xinhua said the satellite will be used for land surveys, crop-yield estimation and disaster relief; it was the 672nd flight of a Long March rocket (Xinhua; Launch Library 2).

Long March 4B: six Yaogan satellites

A Long March 4B lifted off from Launch Area 94 at Jiuquan at 3:25 p.m. EDT Friday (19:25 UTC) with Yaogan-53 07 to 09 and Yaogan-56 19 to 21, which reached their preset orbits. Xinhua said they will be used mainly for scientific experiments, land and resources surveys, crop-yield estimation and disaster prevention; it was the 673rd Long March mission (Xinhua; Launch Library 2).

Falcon 9: Starlink 15-15

Falcon 9 launched from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg at 6:48 p.m. EDT Friday (22:48 UTC), following three scrubs earlier in the week, Spaceflight Now reported. Booster B1063 was on its 36th flight (NASASpaceflight), and SpaceX posted at 7:51 p.m.: "Deployment of 27 @Starlink satellites confirmed" (post).

Long March 8A: SatNet LEO Group 28

A Long March 8A lifted off from Commercial Launch Complex 1 at Wenchang, Hainan, at 10:03 p.m. EDT Saturday (02:03 UTC Sunday), 13 minutes after the time listed in Thursday's issue, with nine low-Earth-orbit communications satellites for the state-owned SatNet constellation operated by China Satellite Network Group, which is to grow to 13,000 satellites (Launch Library 2).

V · Industry & Regulation

Artemis III will test two landers in Earth orbit

NASA laid out a 2027 low-Earth-orbit mission for Orion, Blue Origin's Blue Moon and SpaceX's Starship.

Artemis

Artemis III, now planned for 2027, will send four astronauts to low Earth orbit for about two weeks to rendezvous and dock with test articles of both crewed lunar landers before astronauts begin landing near the Moon's south pole in 2028, NASA said Friday. The crew is NASA's Randy Bresnik, Frank Rubio and Andre Douglas and ESA's Luca Parmitano (NASA; NASA blog). Asked whether NASA was keeping options to launch Blue Moon Mark 2 on another rocket, Artemis program manager Jeremy Parsons and Administrator Jared Isaacman said Blue Origin is exceeding expectations and there are no plans to do so, Spaceflight Now reported (paraphrased).

Blue Origin will launch its Blue Moon Mark 2 test article first, to a circular orbit about 273 miles (237 nautical miles) high, where it can wait up to 30 days. Days later, Orion will launch on the Space Launch System from Kennedy Space Center into an 18-by-1,122-mile orbit and climb to a circular orbit of about 345 miles before docking with Blue Moon, where the crew will spend about two days inside the lander. A Starship Version 3 test article will then launch from Florida into a 206-nautical-mile orbit; Orion will dock at its nose for up to two hours, and the crew will not enter it. Orion will then have up to five more days in orbit before splashing down in the Pacific off California (NASA). "We're launching three spacecraft on three different rockets and testing how they operate together — something NASA has never attempted," Parsons said in the agency's release.

Blue Origin

The final segment of the Launch Complex 36A tower was lifted into place on Saturday (NASASpaceflight). The same day, a New Glenn second stage, GS-2, was being loaded aboard NASA's Pegasus barge for shipment to Stennis Space Center for a static test fire (Spaceflight Now; NASASpaceflight).

Starship

Beyond Booster 22's static fire, Starship Flight 16 is being readied as the vehicle's first launch from Florida, NASASpaceflight reported. Ship 43, for Flight 16, was returning from the Massey's test site early Monday, with Ship 42, for Flight 15, taking its place (NASASpaceflight).

Rocket Lab

"With more than 100 launches in backlog (and counting), we're building a new state-of-the-art test facility to accelerate static fire stack testing for Electron and HASTE rockets," Rocket Lab posted Thursday evening. On Friday it said the Neutron team "hit some big milestones this week" at its Neutron factory in Maryland (post).

Quick Hit

Hera. "Another successful step on Hera's approach to Didymos and Dimorphos," the European Space Agency posted Friday of its asteroid probe.

Europe and India

Arianespace named Filipe de Oliveira, who has more than 20 years in the space industry, as chief commercial officer and a member of its executive committee on Monday (post). ESA said its Argonaut lunar lander will deliver cargo and technology to the Moon's surface, "starting with its first mission from 2030" (post), and that astronaut Sophie Adenot will hold her first in-person press conference since returning from the station at its Paris headquarters on November 2 at 5:30 a.m. EST (post). ISRO will hold the space science and technology session of ESTIC 2026 at Bharat Mandapam in New Delhi on October 29 (post).

Comment deadlines

New Glenn at SLC-36. Comments on raising New Glenn's cap from 12 to 50 launches a year close Friday, October 30 (notice, docket FAA-2026-9841).

Part 450. Comments on five proposed changes to the commercial launch and reentry licensing rule are due November 4 (docket FAA-2026-9935).

FCC space licensing. The Federal Communications Commission's further notice of proposed rulemaking on Space Modernization for the 21st Century (SB Docket No. 25-306) was published Monday; comments are due November 25 and replies December 28 (Federal Register).

Starbase. Scoping comments on up to 456 Starship launches a year at Starbase are due November 30, with meetings November 17 to 19 (Federal Register).

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 250 orbital launch attempts through Saturday night's Long March 8A, with 243 successes. SpaceX has flown 121, all successful, followed by China with 76, Rocket Lab with 17 and Russia with 13; Europe has flown seven, other U.S. providers nine, and India and Japan three each. GCAT's updated list puts the total through October 21 at 245; Thursday's issue, which added Launch Library 2 entries to GCAT's October 15 count, reported 246.

The failures

According to GCAT, seven launches have failed this year: India's PSLV on January 12; China's Long March 3B on January 16, Ceres-2 on January 17, Tianlong-3 on April 3 and Long March 7A on August 10; Japan's Kairos on March 5; and Blue Origin's New Glenn on April 19, recorded as a partial failure.

October so far

Nine of this month's 18 orbital launches were SpaceX Falcons (Launch Library 2). Vandenberg has hosted five of them, more than any other site, and Cape Canaveral and Kennedy four between them. China has flown six, from Wenchang, Jiuquan, Taiyuan and a sea platform off Rizhao, while South Korea's Nuri, Japan's H3 and Rocket Lab's Electron account for the other three (Launch Library 2).

The week ahead

Three orbital launches have specific dates between Monday and November 2 in Launch Library 2: two SpaceX Falcon 9s from Vandenberg and ISRO's GSLV. If all fly on schedule, October would end with 21 orbital launches and SpaceX would reach 123 for the year.

Both Vandenberg Starlinks fly on veteran boosters. Starlink 15-15 was B1063's 36th flight (NASASpaceflight), and B1082 is set for its 26th on Starlink 15-16 (Spaceflight Now). The first firm November dates belong to two more Vandenberg flights on Thursday, November 5: Starlink 15-21 at 12:14 a.m. EST, on B1103's seventh flight (Spaceflight Now), and NROL-195 for the National Reconnaissance Office at 5:34 p.m. EST (Launch Library 2). Later in the month, Arianespace's VA271 is set to carry 36 Amazon Leo satellites on the 10th Ariane 6, no earlier than November 23 (Spaceflight Now).
