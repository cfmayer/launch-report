The Week in Launch

Three launches since Monday, Dragon arrives today, and a Space Force flight tonight

SpaceX launched twice in ten hours, sending 27 Starlink satellites up from California on Monday night and NASA's CRS-35 cargo Dragon toward the International Space Station (ISS) on Tuesday morning. On Wednesday, China's LandSpace put ten satellites in orbit on a methane-fueled Zhuque-2E, eight of them for an orbital computing constellation. That brings 2026 to 242 orbital launch attempts, according to GCAT.

Dragon is due to dock at about 11 a.m. EDT today, and a Falcon 9 launches the classified USSF-481 mission from Vandenberg at 7:44 p.m. EDT. Japan's H3 carries the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe on Monday. The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) opened an environmental review of up to 456 Starship launches a year from Starbase, Texas, and ISRO's NVS-03 window opens today with no launch date announced.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

Three launches, three successes

Two Falcon 9s, including the first Starlink flight in more than three weeks, and a Chinese commercial rocket.

Falcon 9: Starlink 15-25

After four date changes, Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, at 8:28 p.m. EDT Monday (00:28 UTC Tuesday) with 27 Starlink V2 Mini satellites, Spaceflight Now reported. Booster B1088 landed on the drone ship Of Course I Still Love You on its 20th flight. It was the first Falcon 9 Starlink launch since September 19, Space.com reported.

Falcon 9: CRS-35

Falcon 9 launched NASA's 35th SpaceX cargo mission from Space Launch Complex 40 at Cape Canaveral at 6:33 a.m. EDT Tuesday, and booster B1096 landed at Landing Zone 40 on its eighth flight, SpaceX posted. Cargo Dragon C211 carries more than 6,300 pounds, including the last pair of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays (NASA).

Zhuque-2E: ten satellites

LandSpace's Zhuque-2E Y4 lifted off from the Dongfeng commercial space innovation pilot zone at Jiuquan at 7:40 p.m. Beijing time Wednesday, 7:40 a.m. EDT, and deployed 10 satellites into their preset orbits, Xinhua reported. Eight belong to ADA Space's "star compute" constellation, planned to reach 2,800 computing satellites, and fly in a dawn-dusk orbit; two are LandTower laser-communication test satellites. It was the 10th flight of the Zhuque-2 family, LandSpace said.

III · Trajectories & Hazard Areas

Where H3's spent stages will fall

JAXA has published the sea areas where MMX's boosters, nose fairing and first stage are expected to come down.

H3 Flight 10 timeline

According to the launch plan, the rocket turns its pitch plane to an azimuth of 90 degrees, due east, shortly after liftoff and flies out over the Pacific. The upper stage fires twice and releases MMX into a Mars transfer orbit 71 minutes after liftoff. The times below assume launch at 4:41:03 a.m. JST on October 20 (3:41:03 p.m. EDT October 19). Tonight's Vandenberg window

The FAA's air traffic operations plan lists SpaceX's TH-2 mission, the USSF-481 launch, from Vandenberg with a primary window of 7:44 p.m. to 12:27 a.m. EDT (23:44–04:27 UTC) tonight and a backup from 7:35 p.m. Friday. Predicted

A Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) upper stage from the February 2022 EOS-04 launch was predicted to reenter at 11:16 p.m. EDT Wednesday, plus or minus 13 hours, and Russia's Cosmos 2572 satellite at 12:46 a.m. EDT Sunday, October 18, plus or minus 28 hours (Aerospace). Both are in near-polar orbits, so they could come down almost anywhere.

The next Russian cargo ship, Progress MS-36, is listed for 12:03 a.m. Eastern Standard Time (EST) on November 24 from Baikonur (Launch Library 2).

V · Industry & Regulation

FAA opens a review of 456 Starship launches a year in Texas

A new environmental study for Starbase joins the Florida reviews and the Part 450 rule changes now out for comment.

Starbase

The FAA will prepare a Programmatic Environmental Impact Statement (PEIS) on SpaceX's proposal to increase Starship-Super Heavy operations at the Boca Chica launch site at Starbase and to add Starship landing and expenditure locations, according to a notice published October 14 (Federal Register, docket FAA-2026-12808). SpaceX proposes up to 100 launches and 200 landings a year at first, then 456 launches and 912 landings, against 25 launches allowed now, Invezz reported. In-person scoping meetings are set for November 17 and 19, a virtual one for November 18, and comments are due November 30 (SpacePolicyOnline).

Florida and Part 450

Starship at SLC-37. Scoping comments on up to 76 Starship launches a year at Cape Canaveral closed Wednesday (notice, docket FAA-2026-10792).

New Glenn at SLC-36. A virtual public meeting on raising New Glenn's cap from 12 to 50 launches a year is set for October 22 from 6 to 8 p.m. EDT; comments close October 30 (notice, docket FAA-2026-9841).

Part 450. Comments on five proposed changes to the licensing rule for U.S. commercial launches and reentries are due November 4, including fully digital license applications (docket FAA-2026-9935) and simpler flight safety analysis (FAA-2026-9936).

Ship 40 back home

Ship 40, which made Starship's first intact ocean splashdown on Flight 13 and was towed to Christmas Island after 24 days adrift, now stands upright in a Megabay at Starbase. SpaceX says it "will provide first-of-its-kind data on a flown Starship heatshield, Raptor engines, avionics, and more" (SpaceX; TeslaNorth).

Quick Hit

Orbital computing. ADA Space's second group of "star compute" satellites, launched Wednesday, will test high-performance computing payloads, laser links and large radiator wings, China Daily reported; a third group is planned for 2026.

Starlink and Ukraine

Ukrainian officials said military Starshield terminals were restricted while moving at 72 to 161 km/h overnight into October 14. Elon Musk said limits on Starshield "are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS," Politico reported.

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 242 orbital launch attempts through October 14, 235 of them successful, including Wednesday's Zhuque-2E. SpaceX has flown 118, all successful, followed by China with 72, Rocket Lab with 16 and Russia with 13. Nine orbital launches have flown so far in October.

Busiest launch sites

Vandenberg Space Force Base in California remains the year's busiest launch site, ahead of Florida's Cape Canaveral and Kennedy Space Center combined (GCAT, through October 14).
