The Week in Launch

Nuri goes four for four, Crew-12 comes home, and a Mars-moon mission heads for the pad

South Korea's Nuri rocket put 14 satellites in orbit on Wednesday, its fourth success in a row. It was the only orbital launch anywhere in the world between Monday and Thursday morning, according to Launch Library 2. A SpaceX Falcon 9 carrying 21 Space Development Agency (SDA) satellites aborted at T-minus one minute on Monday and is now set for 3:29 a.m. EDT Saturday. On Thursday, the Crew-12 astronauts splashed down off Los Angeles after 237 days on the International Space Station (ISS).

The next two weeks are busier: a Long March 12 from Hainan on Friday, three more Falcon 9 launches, and NASA's 35th Commercial Resupply Services cargo flight (CRS-35) on Tuesday. Then, on October 19, Japan's H3 rocket launches the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe, which is designed to return samples from the Martian moon Phobos. Three old rocket stages are predicted to fall back to Earth on Saturday. In Washington, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has proposed loosening five launch-safety rules.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

Nuri goes four for four

South Korea's homegrown rocket delivered five research microsatellites and nine CubeSats; one CubeSat did not separate.

Nuri flight 5: success from Naro

The three-stage Nuri, also called the Korea Space Launch Vehicle-II (KSLV-II), lifted off on schedule from Naro Space Center at 12:25 p.m. Korea Standard Time (KST) on Wednesday, October 7, which was 11:25 p.m. EDT Tuesday (03:25 UTC). It carried five NEONSAT Earth-observation microsatellites, numbers 2 to 6, built by the Korea Advanced Institute of Science and Technology (KAIST), each under 100 kg, plus 10 CubeSats, bound for a sun-synchronous orbit about 575 km up, Yonhap reported. All five NEONSATs had contacted ground stations by 5:52 p.m. KST, according to the Korea Herald.

Nine of the 10 CubeSats deployed. The tenth, PERSAT02 from the startup Quaternion, failed to separate (Yonhap). The Korea Aerospace Research Institute (KARI) runs the program with Hanwha Aerospace. Nuri now has four straight successes and a record of four out of five, after its first flight failed in 2021 (Space.com). The next flight, carrying NEONSATs 7 to 11, is planned for the second half of 2027. The government has added five more Nuri flights through 2032, budgeted at 328 billion won (Korea Herald).

Falcon 9 aborts at T-minus one minute

SpaceX's first try at the SDA's Tranche 1 Transport Layer A mission from Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, ended on Monday, October 5. The countdown stopped just after T-minus one minute, as the vehicle entered startup. No reason was given, Spaceflight Now reported. A second attempt on October 6 was waved off, and the launch then moved to October 8 and October 9. In a post on X on Wednesday evening, SpaceX said it was now targeting October 10:

"The rocket and payload remain healthy, and additional time allows for double checks on vehicle systems and range deconfliction"
SpaceX · @SpaceX, Wed Oct 7, 8:34 p.m. EDT

Crew-12 splashes down after 237 days

SpaceX's Crew Dragon Freedom undocked from the space-facing port of the station's Harmony module at 8:05 a.m. EDT Wednesday, NASASpaceflight reported. It splashed down in the Pacific off Los Angeles at 11:34 a.m. EDT Thursday and was recovered by the ship Shannon, NASA said. Aboard were NASA astronauts Jessica Meir and Jack Hathaway, European Space Agency (ESA) astronaut Sophie Adenot and Roscosmos cosmonaut Andrey Fedyaev. The mission lasted 237 days, and Meir now has 440 days in space across her flights. It was Freedom's fifth mission. SpaceX posted, "Splashdown of Dragon confirmed!"

What's flying

Long March 12. The launch was added to schedules after airspace notices appeared. If it flies, it would be the fifth Long March 12 of the year, on a southeasterly track out of Wenchang (NASASpaceflight). The window runs from 3:20 to 3:49 p.m. EDT.

Quick Hit

Starship. NASASpaceflight's unofficial schedule puts Flight 15 from Starbase Pad 2 NET November 8 and Flight 16, possibly from Kennedy's Launch Complex 39A, NET December 10.

SDA Transport Layer. This is the fourth of six Tranche 1 Transport Layer launches. The satellites are part of the SDA's data transport layer, and 63 Tranche 1 satellites are already up, according to Spaceflight Now. The launch time is instantaneous.

CRS-35. The 35th SpaceX cargo flight to the ISS uses Cargo Dragon C211 on its fourth flight. It carries the final pair of ISS Roll-Out Solar Arrays, along with hardware for making artificial retinas, 3D heart-tissue models, glass-formation samples and brain organoids for research on neurodegenerative diseases (NASA).

H3 / MMX. JAXA's probe is designed to bring Phobos samples back to Earth in Japanese fiscal year 2031, which would be the first sample return from the Mars system (JAXA launch plan). If the launch slips, the reserve period runs to November 7, with a new launch time set for each day. Liftoff is 4:41:03 a.m. Japan Standard Time (JST) on Tuesday, October 20.

NVS-03. India's NavIC navigation system has only three working satellites, one short of the four it needs for standalone positioning, Union minister Jitendra Singh said. ISRO's previous navigation satellite, NVS-02, was stranded after its 2025 launch because a signal failed to reach a pyro valve in its orbit-raising engine (Times of India).

Vulcan returns. "I can say we're weeks away from launching on Vulcan," Amazon Leo chief Rajeev Badyal told Ars Technica. He said an Arianespace launch would follow, with at least one more Vulcan this year, and that Vulcan's nozzle issue "has been fixed."

Further out

China's Shenzhou-24 crew launch looks likely around November, according to SpaceNews.

V · Industry & Regulation

FAA proposes five changes to launch licensing

The proposals target Part 450, the licensing rule for U.S. commercial launches and reentries in effect since 2021. Separate environmental reviews could allow many more Starship and New Glenn launches from Florida.

Part 450 proposals

Five notices of proposed rulemaking appeared in the Federal Register on October 5, with comments due November 4. They would simplify how operators show compliance with flight safety analysis (docket FAA-2026-9936); add an exception to lightning-hazard rules (FAA-2026-9940); delete the word "hypothetical" from far-field blast analysis, so it covers existing windows only (FAA-2026-9941); simplify physical containment requirements (FAA-2026-9934); and allow fully digital license applications (FAA-2026-9935).

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced the package, the first output of the Department of Transportation's Space Policy Administration, Coordination, and Execution (SPACE) Task Force, Payload reported. The changes are meant to help reach the White House goal of 1,000 commercial launches and reentries a year by 2030. Acting Deputy FAA Administrator Liam McKenna called them "just the beginning."

Environmental reviews in Florida

Starship at SLC-37. The FAA is preparing a Supplemental Environmental Impact Statement (SEIS) on SpaceX's plan for up to 76 Starship launches and 152 landings a year at Cape Canaveral. Scoping comments are due Wednesday, October 14 (notice, docket FAA-2026-10792).

New Glenn at SLC-36. A draft Environmental Assessment (EA) covers Blue Origin's request to raise New Glenn's cap from 12 to 50 launches a year, with no new construction. A virtual public meeting is set for October 22 from 6 to 8 p.m. EDT, and comments close October 30 (notice, docket FAA-2026-9841).

Amazon Leo nears service

Amazon has built its 1,000th Leo broadband satellite and has 396 in orbit. It plans to start initial service in 2026, the company told Ars Technica in an interview published Thursday.

Quick Hit

Terran R. The FAA has adopted a Finding of No Significant Impact and Record of Decision for Relativity Space's Terran R program at Cape Canaveral, under docket FAA-2026-12046 (notice).

Europe

Themis. ArianeGroup and SSC Space completed a second wet dress rehearsal of the Themis reusable-rocket demonstrator at Esrange, Sweden, on September 29. The vehicle was filled with liquid nitrogen instead of propellant. ESA announced the test on October 7 (European Spaceflight). The European Union has extended its €42.8 million SALTO (reuSable strAtegic space Launcher Technologies & Operations) project, which funds Themis's first two hop tests, to December 31, 2026 (European Spaceflight).

HyImpulse. The German startup received a launch operator license from the UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) on October 1. The license covers its SR75 suborbital rocket from SaxaVord, Scotland, but the flight has slipped to spring 2027. The company cited the remaining licensing work and autumn weather (European Spaceflight).

India

Agnikul Cosmos fired its single-piece, 3D-printed Agnite semi-cryogenic engine. The test included thrust sweeps and a run "well beyond the max. burn time of some of our ascent phase engines," as the company prepares for its Agnibaan Mission-02 (India Today).

Quick Hit

Blue Origin in Texas. Blue Origin announced Constellation Park in Hutto, Texas, a 1.3 million-square-foot site with 2,000 jobs, for its TeraWave and Quartz programs.

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 236 orbital launch attempts through October 2, 229 of them successful. Nuri's October 7 flight brings the year to 237. The U.S. leads with 125 attempts, followed by China with 70, Rocket Lab's Electron from New Zealand with 15 (plus one from the U.S.) and Russia with 13.

Busiest launch sites

Vandenberg Space Force Base in California is the year's busiest launch site, ahead of Florida's Cape Canaveral and Kennedy Space Center combined. March and April were the busiest months, with 30 orbital launch attempts each (GCAT, through October 2).

The failures

According to GCAT, seven launches have failed this year: India's Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) on January 12; China's Long March 3B on January 16, Ceres-2 on January 17, Tianlong-3 on April 3 and Long March 7A on August 10; Japan's Kairos on March 5; and Blue Origin's New Glenn on April 19, recorded as a partial failure. A New Glenn that exploded on its pad on May 29 is not counted as a launch attempt.

China's fourth-quarter push

China reached 70 orbital launch attempts on October 1. To pass 100 in a year for the first time, it needs at least 31 more in the fourth quarter, after 18, 26 and 26 in the first three, SpaceNews reported. Possible first flights include Orienspace's Gravity-2, iSpace's Hyperbola-3, which would attempt first-stage recovery, and Zenk Space's Zhihang-1.
