The Week in Launch

India's NVS-03 and the 700th Falcon 9 close out October

Two orbital launches flew worldwide since Thursday's issue. The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) put the NVS-03 navigation satellite into orbit on a Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV) Friday afternoon, and SpaceX launched Starlink 15-18 from Vandenberg just after midnight Saturday, the 700th Falcon 9 flight. That brings 2026 to 253 orbital launch attempts, 246 of them successful, according to GCAT, and October ended with every launch a success.

Nine more launches have dates in the coming week, starting with Rocket Lab's 99th Electron at 11:41 a.m. Monday. The U.S. Space Force mission USSF-432 has moved to Tuesday night in Launch Library 2. On the International Space Station, Thursday's spacewalk installed a new roll-out solar array, and the final power-upgrade spacewalk is set for November 5.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

GSLV puts NVS-03 in orbit; Falcon 9 flies for the 700th time

ISRO's fourth launch of the year and SpaceX's 123rd were both successful.

GSLV: NVS-03

"LIFT-OFF! GSLV-F18 carrying NVS-03 lifts off from Satish Dhawan Space Centre, SHAR, Sriharikota," ISRO posted at 2:21 p.m. EDT Friday (18:21 UTC), 11:51 p.m. India time. Thirty-two minutes later it added: "Mission Success! GSLV-F18 has successfully placed NVS-03 in the intended orbit." The rocket flew from the Second Launch Pad (NASASpaceflight).

NVS-03, also designated IRNSS-1L, is a replacement satellite for India's regional navigation system, NavIC (Launch Library 2). GCAT records an initial transfer orbit of 178 by 37,896 kilometers, inclined 20.7 degrees. It was India's fourth orbital launch of 2026; the first, a Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle on January 12, failed.

Falcon 9: Starlink 15-18

"LAUNCH! SpaceX Falcon 9 B1093-18 launches Starlink Group 15-18 from SLC-4E, Vandenberg," NASASpaceflight posted just after midnight. Liftoff was at 12:04 a.m. EDT Saturday (04:04 UTC), 9:04 p.m. Friday local time (Spaceflight Now); Thursday's issue had listed the 11:48 p.m. start of the FAA airspace window. Booster B1093 was on its 18th flight, and the rocket headed for an orbit inclined 70 degrees (GCAT).

The booster landed on the droneship Of Course I Still Love You, the 675th Falcon booster landing, and SpaceX confirmed deployment of the 27 satellites at 1:08 a.m. It was the 700th Falcon 9 flight and SpaceX's 11th and final orbital launch of October (Spaceflight Now). No other orbital launches took place worldwide between Thursday's issue and Monday morning (Launch Library 2).

IV · Reentries & ISS Traffic

Nine objects come down, including a 1969 Agena fragment

CelesTrak lists three Starlinks, an Electron upper stage and the Rassvet-3 satellite that was still up on Thursday among objects decayed since October 28.

Station traffic: one spacewalk left

NASA astronaut Anil Menon and Canadian Space Agency astronaut Joshua Kutryk "completed a spacewalk at 3:08 p.m. EDT today after installing a roll-out solar array for power generation," NASA's space station account posted Thursday. "They will conduct a final power upgrade spacewalk on Thursday, Nov. 5." The spacewalk was underway by 8:13 a.m. (NASA), and the array started to unroll during the afternoon (Spaceflight Now). The Expedition 75 crew spent Friday on space-crop and technology research (NASA).

The next visitors are Progress MS-36 at 12:03 a.m. November 24 from Baikonur, China's Shenzhou 24 to Tiangong NET November 25, with the first astronaut from Pakistan expected aboard, and Cygnus NG-25, now listed for 11 a.m. November 29 (Launch Library 2).

V · Industry & Regulation

Rocket Lab's 100th Electron leaves the factory

Neutron's upper stage reached its Virginia pad, Blue Origin rebuilt its New Glenn tower, and both Flight 15 Starship vehicles finished their static fires.

Rocket Lab

"Triple digits unlocked," Rocket Lab posted Thursday. "The 100th Electron has left the factory, headed for launch." Monday's mission is its 99th, "our last double-digit launch," the company said Sunday. "Neutron's Stage 2 and Thrust Module have arrived to Launch Complex 3," it posted Friday, "another major step forward for the team that brings us closer to Neutron's debut"; the hardware came by sea from Middle River, Maryland, to the Mid-Atlantic Regional Spaceport at Wallops, Virginia (Rocket Lab). Rocket Lab Robotics also unveiled a robotic arm developed with NASA that "can walk across spacecraft, use tools, and perform repairs and upgrades in orbit"; it launches on NASA's Fly Foundational Robots mission in late 2027 (post).

Blue Origin

Blue Origin is targeting a return to flight for New Glenn by the end of 2026 after the May hot-fire anomaly at Launch Complex 36A, NASASpaceflight reported. Chief executive Dave Limp confirmed on October 24 that all nine modules of the pad's Vehicle Access Tower are repaired and restacked, and crews made the first major concrete pour for a second pad, LC-36B, using a convoy of 450 trucks. Hull welding of the Blue Moon Mark 2 test article that will fly on Artemis III is expected to finish by January 2027. The company also introduced ATLAS, "the first U.S. Department of Labor registered apprenticeship built exclusively for space manufacturing" (Blue Origin).

Under NASA's revised Artemis III plan, the Mark 2 test article will launch on New Glenn to low Earth orbit ahead of Orion, and the crew will spend two days docked to it, checking life support, avionics and manual controls. NASA will choose between Blue Moon and SpaceX's Starship for the Artemis IV landing, targeted for 2028, based on how both perform on Artemis III (NASASpaceflight).

United Launch Alliance

Vulcan's return to flight with 40 Amazon Leo satellites remains "planned for Friday, Nov. 13, pending range approval" (ULA). It will fly with redesigned solid rocket booster nozzles (Spaceflight Now).

Starship

Booster 22 fired all 33 Raptor engines for a full-duration 15-second static fire on October 25, and Ship 42 completed a single-engine burn simulating a deorbit on October 26 and a 60-second, six-engine static fire on October 27, finishing engine testing for Flight 15, NASASpaceflight reported. Ship 43, for Flight 16, completed cryogenic proof tests and practice catches with the Pad 2 tower arms.

"We expect Starship to fly weekly at some point in the first half next year and then second half next year, twice weekly or maybe even more frequently by the end of next year," SpaceX founder Elon Musk said on the company's third-quarter earnings call, adding that SpaceX plans to try to catch the Starship upper stage on Flight 15, expected in mid-November (Spaceflight Now).

Falcon slows down

SpaceX's 11 launches in October compare with 13 in each of January, June, July and August. The October 27 Starlink 15-16 launch was the first time since July 2025 that SpaceX's pace fell below 150 orbital launches in a 365-day period, down from a peak of 171 in December 2025, and SpaceX stopped flying Starlink missions from Florida on Falcon 9 in August, Spaceflight Now reported. Of Course I Still Love You, the Pacific droneship that caught B1093 on Saturday, has now hosted 231 landings.

Quick Hit

Leo 2 is ready. A second Vulcan is already assembled for the Vulcan Leo 2 mission (Spaceflight Now).

Comment deadlines

Part 450. Comments on five proposed changes to the commercial launch and reentry licensing rule are due Wednesday, November 4 (docket FAA-2026-9935).

FCC space licensing. Comments on the Federal Communications Commission's Space Modernization for the 21st Century further notice (SB Docket No. 25-306) are due November 25 and replies December 28 (Federal Register).

Starbase. Scoping comments on up to 456 Starship launches a year at Starbase are due November 30, with meetings November 17 to 19 (Federal Register).

Every one of October's 21 launches succeeded, and 11 of them were SpaceX Falcons.

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 253 orbital launch attempts through Saturday's Starlink 15-18, with 246 successes. SpaceX has flown 123, all successful, followed by China with 76, Rocket Lab with 17 and Russia with 13; other U.S. providers have flown nine, Europe seven, India four and Japan three. GCAT counts Japan's October 19 launch of the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) probe as a deep-space launch, outside these totals.

The failures

According to GCAT, seven launches have failed this year: India's PSLV on January 12; China's Long March 3B on January 16, Ceres-2 on January 17, Tianlong-3 on April 3 and Long March 7A on August 10; Japan's Kairos on March 5; and Blue Origin's New Glenn on April 19, recorded as a partial failure. Galactic Energy's Ceres-2 is now listed to fly again on November 6 (Launch Library 2).

October in full

Eleven of October's 21 launches were SpaceX Falcons, the last of them Saturday's Starlink 15-18 (GCAT). Vandenberg hosted seven of them, more than any other site, and Cape Canaveral and Kennedy four between them. China flew six, and South Korea's Nuri, Japan's H3, Rocket Lab's Electron and India's GSLV account for the other four.
