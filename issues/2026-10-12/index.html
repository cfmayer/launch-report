The Week in Launch

The SDA batch finally flies, China adds 27th SatNet group, and three old stages come home

A SpaceX Falcon 9 carried 21 Space Development Agency (SDA) satellites to orbit from California at 3:39 a.m. EDT Saturday, five days after an abort at T-minus 58 seconds. Eleven hours earlier, at 3:27 p.m. EDT Friday, a Long March 12 lifted off from Hainan with the 27th group of China's SatNet low-orbit internet satellites. Those were the world's only two orbital launches since Thursday, according to Launch Library 2, bringing 2026 to 239 attempts.

All three rocket stages predicted to reenter on Saturday, including a Delta 2 stage launched in 1990, did so, according to CelesTrak. This week brings a Starlink launch Monday night, NASA's CRS-35 cargo flight to the International Space Station (ISS) on Tuesday and a U.S. Space Force mission on Thursday, with Japan's H3 and its Mars-moon probe a week out. Elon Musk said Starship's next flight is four to six weeks away and will try to catch the ship itself.

I · Recent Launches & Returns

Two launches, two successes

Falcon 9 cleared a week of delays for the SDA; China's Long March 12 flew its fifth mission of the year.

Falcon 9: SDA Tranche 1 Transport Layer A

Falcon 9 lifted off from Space Launch Complex 4E at Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, at 3:39 a.m. EDT on Saturday, October 10 (07:39 UTC), with 21 Northrop Grumman-built satellites for the SDA's Tranche 1 Transport Layer, Spaceflight Now reported. Booster B1103, on its sixth flight, landed at Landing Zone 4 about 7.5 minutes after liftoff.

The first attempt, on October 5, stopped 58 seconds before liftoff after onboard computers flagged "some off-nominal data on Stage Two." SpaceX then moved the date a day at a time. "Additional time throughout the week allowed for double checks on vehicle systems and range deconfliction," SpaceX launch commentator Atticus Vadera said. Spaceflight Now said the range conflict is believed to have been a missile test from Vandenberg on Friday; the Federal Aviation Administration's (FAA) advisory had listed a Northrop Grumman MK21-2B launch from the base that morning. It was the fourth of six Tranche 1 Transport Layer launches.

Long March 12: SatNet LEO Group 27

China launched a Long March 12 from the commercial launch site in Hainan at 3:27 a.m. Beijing time on Saturday, 3:27 p.m. EDT Friday, and placed the 27th group of low-orbit internet satellites into the preset orbit, Xinhua reported. The flight from Commercial Launch Complex 2 at Wenchang was within the 3:20–3:49 p.m. EDT window that airspace notices had shown, and China Aerospace Science and Technology Corp. (CASC) did not release the number of satellites. Launch Library 2 records it as a success. SatNet, also known as Guowang, is China's state-run broadband megaconstellation.

V · Industry & Regulation

Starship comments due Wednesday; launch rules open to November 4

The proposals target Part 450, the licensing rule for U.S. commercial launches and reentries in effect since 2021. Separate environmental reviews could allow many more Starship and New Glenn launches from Florida.

Part 450 proposals

Five notices of proposed rulemaking appeared in the Federal Register on October 5, with comments due November 4. They would simplify how operators show compliance with flight safety analysis (docket FAA-2026-9936); add an exception to lightning-hazard rules (FAA-2026-9940); delete the word "hypothetical" from far-field blast analysis, so it covers existing windows only (FAA-2026-9941); simplify physical containment requirements (FAA-2026-9934); and allow fully digital license applications (FAA-2026-9935).

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy announced the package, the first output of the Department of Transportation's Space Policy Administration, Coordination, and Execution (SPACE) Task Force, Payload reported. The changes are meant to help reach the White House goal of 1,000 commercial launches and reentries a year by 2030. Acting Deputy FAA Administrator Liam McKenna called them "just the beginning."

Environmental reviews in Florida

Starship at SLC-37. The FAA is preparing a Supplemental Environmental Impact Statement (SEIS) on SpaceX's plan for up to 76 Starship launches and 152 landings a year at Cape Canaveral. Scoping comments are due this Wednesday, October 14 (notice, docket FAA-2026-10792).

New Glenn at SLC-36. A draft Environmental Assessment (EA) covers Blue Origin's request to raise New Glenn's cap from 12 to 50 launches a year, with no new construction. A virtual public meeting is set for October 22 from 6 to 8 p.m. EDT, and comments close October 30 (notice, docket FAA-2026-9841).

Amazon Leo nears service

Amazon has built its 1,000th Leo broadband satellite and has 396 in orbit, and it plans initial service in 2026, the company told Ars Technica last week. Its next launches are the Vulcan and Ariane 64 flights in the schedule above.

Quick Hit

Terran R. The FAA has adopted a Finding of No Significant Impact and Record of Decision for Relativity Space's Terran R program at Cape Canaveral, under docket FAA-2026-12046 (notice).

Europe

Themis. ArianeGroup and SSC Space completed a second wet dress rehearsal of the Themis reusable-rocket demonstrator at Esrange, Sweden, on September 29. The vehicle was filled with liquid nitrogen instead of propellant. ESA announced the test on October 7 (European Spaceflight). The European Union has extended its €42.8 million SALTO (reuSable strAtegic space Launcher Technologies & Operations) project, which funds Themis's first two hop tests, to December 31, 2026 (European Spaceflight).

HyImpulse. The German startup received a launch operator license from the UK Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) on October 1. The license covers its SR75 suborbital rocket from SaxaVord, Scotland, but the flight has slipped to spring 2027. The company cited the remaining licensing work and autumn weather (European Spaceflight).

India

Agnikul Cosmos fired its single-piece, 3D-printed Agnite semi-cryogenic engine. The test included thrust sweeps and a run "well beyond the max. burn time of some of our ascent phase engines," as the company prepares for its Agnibaan Mission-02 (India Today).

Quick Hit

Blue Origin in Texas. Blue Origin announced Constellation Park in Hutto, Texas, a 1.3 million-square-foot site with 2,000 jobs, for its TeraWave and Quartz programs.

The count

Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT) records 239 orbital launch attempts through October 10, 232 of them successful, including Friday's Long March 12 and Saturday's Falcon 9. SpaceX has flown 116, all successful, followed by China with 71, Rocket Lab's Electron from New Zealand with 15 (plus one from the U.S.) and Russia with 13.

Busiest launch sites

Vandenberg Space Force Base in California is the year's busiest launch site, ahead of Florida's Cape Canaveral and Kennedy Space Center combined. March and April were the busiest months, with 30 orbital launch attempts each (GCAT, through October 10).

The failures

According to GCAT, seven launches have failed this year: India's Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle (PSLV) on January 12; China's Long March 3B on January 16, Ceres-2 on January 17, Tianlong-3 on April 3 and Long March 7A on August 10; Japan's Kairos on March 5; and Blue Origin's New Glenn on April 19, recorded as a partial failure. A New Glenn that exploded on its pad on May 29 is not counted as a launch attempt.

China's fourth-quarter push

China reached 71 orbital launch attempts with Friday's Long March 12. To pass 100 in a year for the first time, it needs at least 30 more by December 31; it flew 18, 26 and 26 in the first three quarters, SpaceNews reported. Possible first flights include Orienspace's Gravity-2, iSpace's Hyperbola-3, which would attempt first-stage recovery, and Zenk Space's Zhihang-1.
